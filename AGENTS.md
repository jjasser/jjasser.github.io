# Blog writing style

These preferences apply when drafting or revising blog posts under `content/post/`.

- Use a natural first-person engineering voice, with practical observations and clear explanations.
- Favor connected paragraphs that carry an observation through to its meaning or the next step.
- Vary sentence length, but avoid strings of short declarative sentences, isolated fragments, and abrupt endings that sound robotic.
- Connect related ideas naturally; do not simply join every short sentence with a conjunction or pad the explanation to make it longer.
- Instead of ending with an isolated result such as “The agent refused” or “The model received a batch,” explain what was observed and why it mattered in the same passage.
- Keep technical details, commands, evidence, and limits precise while improving the prose. Never invent test results or imply that proposed defenses have been validated.
- Use earlier series posts for continuity, while giving these newer preferences priority over their sentence cadence.

- Aim for about a 10-minute read for a full technical post, using Hugo’s actual reading-time estimate as a check. Preserve the walkthrough, evidence, and important caveats by cutting repetition and excess setup before cutting substance.
- When improving flow, keep the prose concise; avoid expanding the post simply to connect sentences.
- Introduce each attack vector with a brief plain-language definition before its walkthrough, and avoid repeating that definition later.
